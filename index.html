Method & interpretation

The objective is within-segment squared error plus penalty × number of breakpoints. Every segment is fitted by its mean. Dynamic programming considers every feasible previous boundary and finds a global minimum of the computed floating-point costs. Exact ties prefer fewer segments, then the earliest final boundary encountered. No greedy splitting or PELT pruning is used.

Segment costs use a centered online variance update. Values are limited to ±1000000, length to 600, minimum segment length to 1–50, and penalty to 0–10¹². The penalty has squared-data units: multiplying every observation by c calls for multiplying the penalty by c² to preserve the objective's relative weighting.

The sample is synthetic, with level changes at 40 and 90. Squared error is sensitive to outliers. A trend or correlated noise can be represented by artificial level changes. This tool does not choose a scientifically justified penalty, handle irregular timestamps, supply uncertainty intervals or predict future observations. Explained variation is descriptive in-sample fit, not out-of-sample accuracy.
